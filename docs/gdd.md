# Banners & Lords — Game Design

## Concept

A tactical RPG with a lightweight grand strategy campaign in a feudal world. Rise from a small company to a landed power through conquest, settlement, trade, and diplomacy.

Visual direction: hand-drawn 2D, a top-down world map, and bust-style soldiers with visible equipment. Major game panels use the full screen.

## Core Loop

Explore and take opportunities → earn resources and recruit soldiers → fight, negotiate, or trade → establish and supply holdings → expand and defend.

## Core Mechanics

- **Campaign:** continuous world-map travel with active pause; resource sites, caravans, patrols, bandits, and rival lords share the map. Roads, terrain, and crossings should make route choice meaningful; hexes belong to tactical battles.
- **Settlement visits:** inspect villages from the map, travel to them, and enter when nearby. Entering stops the party and holds campaign time; leaving restores the previous pause state. Arrival alone does not enter a settlement.
- **Inventory:** weapons and armor are individual items with quantity one; food and materials form shared stacks without food batches. A full-screen view keeps three columns: counterparty inventory on the left, character/equipment in the center, and player inventory on the right. Outside trade the left column stays blank without shifting the layout. Items use compact rows and descriptive tooltips; category icons remain above the character. Head, body, hands, feet, main-hand, and off-hand equipment updates appearance; replacement or removal returns items to the party inventory.
- **Combat:** turn-based hex battles with terrain, action points, fatigue, armor, and permanent losses. Experienced soldiers are valuable and replaceable only at a cost.
- **Expansion:** settle undeveloped land or conquer existing holdings. Independent settlement requires substantial resources and preparation; land ownership is a progression milestone.
- **Economy:** villages produce resources and recruits; castles depend on deliveries and provide defense and elite training; cities process goods, generate income, and consume food. Supply routes must be protected.
- **Politics:** lords have distinct traits and remember favors and betrayals. Resource shortages, borders, and relative strength influence trade, alliances, passage rights, and war.

## Dynasties and Characters

- Each significant noble character belongs to a dynasty: a family with a name, coat of arms, and head. One dynasty can include multiple lords, knights, ladies, and descendants; commoners do not require dynasty membership.
- Characters have individual traits, relationships, and roles. Multiple members of one dynasty can lead separate parties; a world-map party represents a group under a character's command.
- Family membership and political allegiance are separate. A character may serve another house while retaining their own dynasty; a kingdom can unite several dynasties.
- The player controls a character within a dynasty. Dynastic continuity connects land, family relationships, and succession across generations.
- Party and Dynasty have separate panels: Party covers the commanded group and its orders; Dynasty covers the family, its head, and members. The commander's entry links to their dynasty.
- Marriage, descendants, and inheritance should eventually allow an eligible successor to continue the campaign after the player's character dies. Detailed succession rules remain open.

## Initial Focus

Prove one small campaign region with production, a delivery route, a tactical encounter, and consequences carried back into the campaign.

Open design decisions: early-game progression, large-battle scale, and exact castle control rules.
