# Banners & Lords — Game Design

## Concept

A tactical RPG with a lightweight grand strategy campaign in a feudal world. Rise from a small company to a landed power through conquest, settlement, trade, and diplomacy.

Visual direction: hand-drawn 2D, a top-down world map, and bust-style soldiers with visible equipment.

## Core Loop

Explore and take opportunities → earn resources and recruit soldiers → fight, negotiate, or trade → establish and supply holdings → expand and defend.

## Core Mechanics

- **Campaign:** continuous world-map travel with active pause; resource sites, caravans, patrols, bandits, and rival lords share the map. Roads, terrain, and crossings should make route choice meaningful; hexes belong to tactical battles.
- **Combat:** turn-based hex battles with terrain, action points, fatigue, armor, and permanent losses. Experienced soldiers are valuable and replaceable only at a cost.
- **Expansion:** settle undeveloped land or conquer existing holdings. Independent settlement requires substantial resources and preparation; land ownership is a progression milestone.
- **Economy:** villages produce resources and recruits; castles depend on deliveries and provide defense and elite training; cities process goods, generate income, and consume food. Supply routes must be protected.
- **Politics:** lords have distinct traits and remember favors and betrayals. Resource shortages, borders, and relative strength influence trade, alliances, passage rights, and war.

## Initial Focus

Prove one small campaign region with production, a delivery route, a tactical encounter, and consequences carried back into the campaign.

Open design decisions: early-game progression, large-battle scale, and exact castle control rules.
